About Me

Blockchains are best understood as infrastructure: networks that let strangers agree on shared state without a central operator. I’m drawn to how that foundation, with its consensus rules and incentive design, shapes everything built above it, and how protocols, networks, and digital assets depend on one another.

In decentralized finance, I find the mechanics the most revealing part: automated market makers replacing order books, lending markets setting rates algorithmically, and liquidity acting as the lifeblood that makes all of it usable. Smart contracts are what turn those ideas into open, composable applications.

I also care about reading the evidence. On-chain activity is public, which makes it possible to study how ecosystems actually behave, and the steady progress of Layer 2 scaling shows how the technology is adapting to real demand. These are areas I keep exploring, and I’m committed to going deeper.
